How much time do you (or someone on your team) lose **copying and pasting data** just to find out whether the company made a profit?

The ritual is familiar: open Stripe or the bank, download the statement, drop it into Excel, fix the columns, build a chart… Two hours later, there’s little energy left for the part that matters: **deciding**.

That’s robot work done by people. And it’s exactly the kind of task an AI integration solves.

## You already have the data. What’s missing are answers.

Having data is easy. Turning it into **useful information** is hard.

Many companies know _how much_ they sold, but not _why_ the margin dropped last week, or which expense is getting out of hand. The answer is in the numbers, but nobody has time to look for it.

## How it works in practice

To test the idea, we built **Finance AI**, an internal project that analyzes financial transactions and returns conclusions, not tables:

1. **Connection:** the system pulls data straight from the payment platform (such as Stripe or Asaas).
2. **Analysis:** the data goes to the AI (OpenAI) with clear context, like that of a demanding financial advisor.
3. **Answer:** instead of a spreadsheet, you get a report in plain language. For example: _“Heads up: your infrastructure costs went up 20% this month because of a spike on server X.”_

## “Can’t I just paste the spreadsheet into ChatGPT?”

You can. But it’s still manual, depends on someone remembering to do it, and exposes company data without control.

An **integration inside your system** works on its own. It can, for example:

- **Send the report** to your inbox every Monday morning.
- **Alert you on WhatsApp** when a goal is hit or an expense goes off track.
- **Filter sensitive data** before any information reaches the AI.

## How much does it cost?

Less than you might think. AI usage is billed by volume and usually weighs little next to the hours your team spends building reports.

What’s truly expensive is to keep deciding on gut feeling, or to lose money because nobody had time to check the cost spreadsheet.

## Where this applies

The same logic works beyond finance:

- **Sales:** which products and clients are pushing results up or down.
- **Support:** which questions keep coming back and where customers get stuck.
- **Operations:** bottlenecks and delays before they become complaints.
